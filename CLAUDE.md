# CLAUDE.md — The Power Hour

Context for Claude Code working in this repo. Read README.md for the user-facing overview.

## What this is

A shareable website for Dan's 12-team Sleeper fantasy league, **NFL Fantasy Championship** (league ID `1312001652454088704`, 2026 season). Leaguemates open it to see power rankings with explanations, a live waiver/trade feed, weekly matchup odds, and "nerd lab" stats. "The Power Hour" is also the name of Dan's league newsletter. Hosted on Vercel from GitHub; every push to the main branch deploys.

League facts the code relies on: half-PPR, 12 teams, starting slots QB, RB, RB, WR, WR, TE, FLEX, FLEX, K, DEF + 5 bench. Regular season ends week 14, playoffs start week 15, top 6 make it. Standings tiebreaker is points for. Waivers are rolling (no FAAB used in practice).

## Architecture

React, built with Vite (`vite.config.js`) into `dist/`, which Vercel builds (`npm ci`, `npm run build`) and serves (`vercel.json`: framework vite, output `dist`). Dependencies: `react` and `react-dom` (19), `html-to-image` (1.11.13, pinned, loaded only when someone shares the team dashboard), and for the build `vite` (8) and `@vitejs/plugin-react`. The pages are React components in `src/`, plain JSX (no TypeScript): the Live page (`src/live`: `LivePage` with `YourWeek`, the weekly list, projected standings and the final-scores view with its reveal button), the power rankings (`src/rankings`), the hero both share (`src/hero`: `Hero` puts the `Carousel`, the page head and the league desk `Talk` together), the Wire (`src/wire`), Games (`src/games`), the Lab (`src/lab`: one page with three sections, personal to league-wide: Your team, the team dashboard from `src/dashboard`; Trades, the trade ideas from `src/trades`, once the Trade lab; League, the charts in `LabPage`, once the Nerd lab. `Lab.jsx` puts one heading over all three, `LabHead` in `parts.jsx`: the title, the section switcher `.lab-tabs` (a tray like the header's tabs, the active section on the amber pill, each segment with a line saying what's in it, such as the trade-idea count; phones show the labels only), the section's own line and, for Trades, the team picker on the right), the charts (`src/charts.jsx`) and the shared bits (`src/shared.jsx`). `app.js` is the frame: it loads the data, computes the league (`build`, `headlines`), writes the headlines as data (the stories, `carouselProps()`), keeps the site's state (`DATA`, `myTeam`, the pages' filters in `wireState`, `tradeState`, `labState`) and the props each page needs (`rankingsProps()`, `liveProps()`; `liveProps` renders `DATA.after` through `withData`), runs the router and the motion, and owns the two overlays that are still plain DOM (the important message, `maybeMessage`, and the final call's reveal, `passThenReveal`/`openReveal`). The pages were moved one at a time, each checked pixel-identical to the site before it. One React root sits on `#app` (`pageRoot` in `src/mount.jsx`): `route()` looks the tab up in `PAGES` (the page component and its props; `D`, `me` and `h` go to every page) and renders it synchronously (`flushSync`, so `reveal()` and view transitions see it) under a fresh key, so a page always mounts anew; what a page keeps when you leave it lives in `app.js`'s state objects and the stores. A page's filters are React state, kept in `app.js`'s state objects through `state`/`onState` (`wireState`, `tradeState`) so they survive leaving the page; the Lab's Trades picker (`TradePicker`, in the Lab's heading) sets your team for the whole site (`onTeam`, `setMyTeam`). Trade ideas are cached per team on the league (`D._ideas`). Games (`src/games`): `GamesPage` keeps which breakdowns are open (and which were ever built, kept for the fold) and does the desktop FLIP glide in a layout effect (off while the page is poured); `MatchupCard` has the odds toggle, `BetLine`, `Fans` and the `Breakdown` (`watchItem` became `Watch`, bolding names by splitting the text); `GameOfTheWeek` has the face-off and the playoff drive (its result is React state). Fan picks live in `src/games/votes.js` (`loadVotes`, called once at startup by `app.js`, `castVote`, and `useVotes` for React, through `useSyncExternalStore`). Rankings (`src/rankings`): `RankingsPage` keeps which rows are open (the jersey pin and the first-open nudge, `ph-opened-rank`, live in its `toggle`) and the you strip's jump; `RankRow` builds its `Report` (manager card, superlatives, reasons, radar and weekly bars through `Chart`, schedule, seeds) the first time it opens; `Poll` and `Futures` are their own components. The hero (`src/hero`): `Hero` is the `.hero` (with `.with-talk` once the desk has a thread to show), holding the `Carousel` (props from `carouselProps()` in `app.js`: the stories, today's date, the hold note, night edition, section labels), the page head each page passes in (`RankHead`, `LiveHead`) and the league desk (`Talk`). The carousel runs itself: dots, arrows, swipe, the phone `fit()`, the 60%-on-screen hold, the pause (kept for the visit) and the video headline (`useClip`, which also watches `visibilitychange` and `html.locked`); it hands the desk `{ at, go }` through a shared ref (`nav`). The desk's state lives outside React in `src/hero/talk.js` (`talk`, `setTalk`, `useTalk`, `talkCall`, `loadTalk`, `storyKeys`) so it outlives a page change; `app.js` sets `talk.week` (`talkWeek()`) and handles the `#join=` claim (`claimTalk`). The important message's replay chip asks `message.ready()` (the HEAD check) and plays through `maybeMessage(true)`. The fan poll's state is `pollStore` (`createStore` in `src/store.js`), set by `loadPoll`/`castPoll` in `app.js`. The ranking rows' entrance and count-ups (`revealRanks`) still run on the React-rendered rows. Helpers the Games page needs from `app.js`: `american`, `half`, `isChamp`/`champSeason` (for `ChampTag`/`ChampBadge` in `src/shared.jsx`), `bull` (`bullInfo`, the money week chip as data) and `moneyWeek`. Formatting and names come from `app.js` as one helpers object (`reactHelpers`: `f1`, `pct`, `ordinal`, `short`, `record`, `first`, `groupSaid`, `pctW`, `nameOf`, `posOf`, `faceSrc`, `nfl`, `shortName`, `teams`), so the React pages read exactly like the rest. Charts are components in `src/charts.jsx` (`BumpChart`, `Scatter`, `Radar`, `WeeklyBars`, `DivergingBars`, `HeatTable`). Pictures in React are `TeamAvatar` (the Sleeper team picture) and `Headshot` (the manager's photo), also in `src/shared.jsx`; each handles its own load error, so never give a React image `data-initials`. Keep a space that sits between words in the same string as them (`{`${name} `}`), not in a text node of its own: separate text nodes kern a hair differently. The pure modules in `lib/` (engine, stories, matchup, trades, labmodels, nightdesk, bullseye, dashboard numbers, newsclock, members) stay plain JS and are imported by React and the API alike; keep them free of DOM and React. The build only bundles, minifies and fingerprints: nothing is translated for older browsers (`target`/`cssTarget` esnext), small files aren't inlined (`assetsInlineLimit: 0`), bundled files land in `dist/bundle/` with hashes in their names, and files the page asks for by name at runtime are copied as they are (`assets/`, `demo/`). `api/` and the `lib/` modules it imports are built by Vercel as functions, as before. Each step was checked pixel-identical to the site before it on every page, desktop and phone. Only `dist/` is public (the repo's own files aren't).

- `index.html`, `styles.css`, `app.js` — the site. The browser never calls the Sleeper API: it loads `/api/league?d=KEY` and `/api/ros?d=KEY`, where KEY is the Central date rolling over at 6am (`morningKey()`), so each is fetched once per morning and shared from the CDN (and kept an hour by the browser, `max-age=3600`: a key's snapshot never changes). Load order: the `<head>` preloads both Latin font files and the stylesheet, and Vite adds the bundle's module script and a modulepreload for each chunk. A plain inline `<script data-early>` dynamically imports `/lib/newsclock.js` (the build points it at its own small chunk) and starts both data requests while the main bundle downloads (`window.__early`, skipped in the demo); `loadLive()` reuses them through `early()` when the URL still matches and sets `window.__early` itself first if they never started, so a request is never made twice.
- `api/league.js` — the morning Sleeper snapshot: state, league, users, rosters, matchups weeks 1–14, transactions weeks 1–current, plus `fetched`. It only answers the current key from `leagueKey()` (any other `?d=` gets a 307 to it) and keeps each snapshot in the store (`once('league', key)`, 3 days), so a deploy (Vercel empties the CDN) never re-reads Sleeper. `X-Snapshot` says `store` or `live`. `max-age=3600, s-maxage=86400`; errors are `no-store`. Also exports `snapshot()` for scripts. Hash routing: `#live` (only during a live week), `#rankings`, `#wire`, `#matchups`, `#lab/team`, `#lab/trades`, `#lab/league` (`route()` splits the hash on `/` into the view and `labState.sub`; plain `#lab` opens the last section this visit, team at first, and the old `#trades` opens `#lab/trades`; either way `history.replaceState` writes the full address, so no extra history entry or hashchange).
- `api/ros.js` — Vercel serverless function (Node 18+, global `fetch`). Scrapes the three ROS sources, fetches Sleeper's `/players/nfl` (~5 MB), matches players to Sleeper IDs, returns `{ updated, sources, weights, players: {id: {n,p,t,i,a}}, values: {id: {v, r, spread, pos, wk, fl, cl, ir, sos, g, rk, prk, wkEst?}}, byes: {TEAM: week}, weekly: {week, sources, proj: {id: [pts, fp, sl]}} }`. Bye weeks come from FantasyPros (`player_bye_week` on every ecrData row). Only the current `morningKey()` is served (others redirect). Sleeper's player map is slimmed to fantasy positions and stored on its own (`once('players', key)`), so it's read at most once per morning; the built body is stored for 3 days, or 1h when a ranking source failed (the retry re-scrapes the rankings and reuses the stored players). Edge-cached 24h (`s-maxage=86400`) and an hour in the browser (`max-age=3600`); if a source failed, 1h at the edge and not at all in the browser. No cron; the only database is the fan-picks store behind `api/votes.js`.
- `lib/weekly.js` — this week's player projections for the matchups, pure: the plain average of FantasyPros' API (`/public/v2/json/nfl/{season}/projections`, `points_half`, env `FANTASYPROS_API_KEY`) and Sleeper's projections endpoint (`api.sleeper.app/projections/nfl/{season}/{week}`, `pts_half_ppr`), whichever has the player. `api/ros.js` fetches both for `nflWeek(morningKey)` (weeks turn over Tuesday; `SEASON_TUESDAY` needs updating each season) and stores each on its own (`once('fpproj'|'slproj', key)`), so it's one Sleeper call and about 23 FantasyPros calls per morning. FantasyPros' free tier returns 10 players a call and answers bursts with 429, so `fpIdsToAsk` picks the likely starters from its ROS ranking (`FP_DEPTH`, by `fpid` from ecrData), one call every `FP_GAP_MS`, with retries. Without the API key it runs on Sleeper alone (no hourly retry). The engine uses `weekly` only when `weekly.week` is the upcoming week: `thisWeekProj` reads it (DraftSharks' ROS `wk` for a player neither has), `muWeek[upcoming]` and the live rest are the projection itself (no scale or points-per-game blend), and each preview's `projA/projB` is `t.setProj`, the set lineup's projection (the best lineup if none is set), the same total `breakdown()` adds up. Floors and ceilings are DraftSharks' scaled by this week's projection over `wk`. `meta.hasWeekly` switches the Games copy.
- `lib/newsclock.js` — the shared clock (page and API): `TZ`, `NEWS_CLOCK` (hold, late pull, night edition, game window), `morningKey`, `leagueKey`, `isOnHold`, `isNightDesk`, `inGameWindow`. The early data script in `index.html` imports it on its own (the build gives it a small chunk), so keep it free of imports.
- `lib/liquid.js` — the cartoon liquid shape, pure, tested by `scripts/test-liquid.mjs`. `buildField(prims, blocks, options)` makes a distance field on a grid (`liquidOptions(W)`: 8px cells, 6 on phones): every card is a rounded box, grown 5px so its contents stay well inside; `planFlow` joins cards with necks (a spanning tree over the shortest gaps, a few extra joins, never across `blocks`, the headings and filters on the grass), and necks, slow side swells and drips are smooth-unioned in, so they meet in real fillets; one soft pass rounds any crease, a little noise keeps edges from being straight, a long slow roll only ever pushes them outward, and holes too small or thin to read as grass get filled (`fillSlivers`). With `band` only that stretch is rasterized; everything is still planned from the whole page, so it's the same shape, just cheaper. `contours` is marching squares (`level` below zero shrinks the shape; `rows` traces one band), `pathOf` turns loops into smooth SVG paths, `toneField` gives one tone's fill (a neck between two tones changes colour at its midpoint; `face` also stops short of the crescent along each bottom edge) and `shineOf` the hard highlights (an inner contour kept where the liquid faces up and left). Everything is seeded by position in the list, so every device draws the same shape.
- `src/liquid/pour.js` — puts the liquid on the page: one SVG (`.liquid`) right after `main`, at `z-index: -1`, so it sits over the grass and the painted rank numbers (`.jersey`, also -1, earlier in the tree) and under everything else. Every `.glass` in `main` becomes a primitive and goes see-through (`[data-liq]`; its tone is the CSS variable `--liq`: `face`, `gold`, `mine`, `board`, `notice`, `good`, `bad`, `toilet`; `--liq: none` keeps a card out, which sticky panels need since the shape can't follow them, and they stay the CSS drop `.glass` still draws, as do the overlays and the kicker card). The footer is a primitive too, the pool (`pool: true`, `tone: 'turf'`) the last cards drain into. Positions come from the offset chain, not `getBoundingClientRect`, so transforms mid-entrance don't move the shape. It redraws synchronously inside a ResizeObserver callback on every card (so an opening row's liquid never lags) and when elements come and go (a MutationObserver that ignores text, so count-ups don't count), and skips the work if nothing moved. Those redraws are `quick`: only the screen and 600px either side, so a row opening costs the same on a tall page, and a full trace follows 180 ms after things go still. The page's total path is one long task of about 60 ms at worst, run only then. `route()` calls `pourPage({ entrance })`: on a tab change the first screen's liquid swells from each card's middle with a little overshoot (the same field traced at a rising `level`). Only a real view change swells (`key !== currentView`). `#liquid-flow` holds a droplet per neck in the tone of the card it left, the only motion at rest, and only the ones near the screen run (an IntersectionObserver on still anchors); reduced motion drops both. The header has its own small body with drips (`drawBar`, inside `.topbar`; on a phone the old `::before` skin still does the fold into the football and the SVG fades with the bar's other children). A row's or card's entrance animates its contents only: never put a transform on `.rank`, it would lift the rank number above the liquid. A new card just needs `.glass`; a new one that moves on its own needs `--liq: none`. The gap between cards is `--flow` (42px, 34 on phones), which the necks need to read as necks. Poured cards have no surface of their own, so nothing should move a card by itself: hover doesn't lift them and the Games page's desktop FLIP is off while poured. A rank row is `noLeft`, so no swell covers its painted number; 10 to 12 are set smaller so both digits show.
- `lib/snapstore.js` — `once(name, key, produce)`: each snapshot kept gzipped in the Upstash store (same env vars as the fan picks) under its clock key, with a lock so simultaneous first visitors share one pull. Without the env vars it falls back to pulling on each CDN miss. `scripts/test-snapshots.mjs` counts Sleeper calls.
- `lib/sources.js` — regex-only parsers (must run in Node without a DOM):
  - FantasyPros: `var ecrData = {...};` embedded in the page HTML (~400 players incl. K/DST).
  - DraftSharks: the page only server-renders 25 rows; the full list is `GET /ros-rankings/load-rows?offset=0&limit=600&...` (HTML `<tr class="player-row">` fragments, includes IDP, re-ranked within offense). Also provides weekly proj pts, floor, ceiling, injury risk %, SOS, projected games.
  - LeagueLogs: server-rendered `<table>`, 200 players incl. DST, no K. Its order nearly mirrors FantasyPros ECR, so it is weighted 0.5.
- `lib/consensus.js` — `normName` + `NICKNAMES` map, `buildIndex(sleeperPlayers)`, `matchRow`, `buildConsensus`. Rank → value is `100 * exp(-0.0115 * (rank-1))`, weighted average across sources (`SOURCE_WEIGHTS`). Missing weekly projections are filled by a per-position linear fit of DraftSharks pts vs value, falling back to `DEFAULT_FIT` if DraftSharks is down.
- `lib/engine.js` — `computeLeague(input)`: pure, no DOM, runs in browser and Node. Produces teams (sorted by power rank), weekly results, all-play, luck, efficiency, ROS best lineup (greedy fixed-then-flex), position-group ranks (`groupScores`, quality over quantity: each starter's weekly projection minus the league baseline at his position, `meta.posBase`, the Nth-best rostered player with N = teams × dedicated slots, so WR24, RB24, QB12, TE12; dedicated slots weigh `GROUP_W.fixed` 1.25 and can go negative, flex use weighs 0.5 then 0.25 and never below zero, so a pile of middling receivers can't outrank a better group one player shorter; the Trade lab scores groups the same way, and tooltips read "+x pts/wk over the WR24 line" via `groupSaid` in `app.js`), power score + rank history, Monte Carlo playoff odds and championship odds (10k sims, seeded; the playoff bracket is simulated on Sleeper's fixed 4/6/8-team bracket, giving `champPct`/`finalPct`; `simulate` runs on team indexes and typed arrays but draws the same random numbers in the same order as the plain version it replaced, and `scripts/test-engine.mjs` checks a fingerprint of the fixture's odds, so a change to it that shifts any probability fails the test), matchup previews, explanations (`explain`), tiers (`tierFor`), transaction feed with ROS value deltas (trades priced over replacement: each net extra player received costs `meta.replacement`, the mean value of the top 3 free agents, so consolidation trades are not penalised; waiver moves stay raw), best available free agents, "divisive" players.
- `lib/stories.js` — the newsroom, pure like the engine. `buildStories(D, {now, dateKey})` scores candidate stories (fresh trades and wire moves, unbeaten/winless/streaks, last week's upsets/blowouts/nail-biters, rank moves, luck, game of the week) and returns `{lead, more}`; a date-seeded jitter rotates near-ties daily. `keyMatchup(D)` picks the game of the week by 0.55 × stakes + 0.45 × closeness, where stakes is the combined playoff-odds swing (`playoffIfWin − playoffIfLoss` from the simulation). `matchupQuips(D)` gives one data-driven one-liner per matchup, no repeated joke within a week.
- `api/votes.js` — fan picks. GET `?week=&voter=` returns `{week, open, counts: {"3-8": {"3": n}}, mine}`; POST `{week, pair, pick, voter}` records a vote (re-voting replaces the device's pick). Storage is Upstash Redis over its REST pipeline API with plain fetch (env `KV_REST_API_URL`/`KV_REST_API_TOKEN` or `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_*`); without them GET returns `{enabled: false}` (200, no console noise), POST returns 503, and the UI hides voting. Server validates against the cached `/api/league` snapshot on its own host (current week, real pair, pick in pair), never Sleeper directly, and closes voting from Thursday 7:15pm Central through Monday or once the snapshot shows points. `VOTES_NOW` env overrides the clock in tests. Soft cap of 3 voters per hashed IP per matchup. Keys `ph:votes:{week}:{pair}` (hash voter→pick) and `ph:net:{week}:{pair}:{iphash}` (set), 120-day TTL. The client keeps its voter id in `localStorage` `ph-voter` and hides the split until you've voted.
- `api/talk.js` — the league desk, one discussion thread per week beside the headlines (a hash of comment id → `{id, uid, text, ref, ts, edited?}`, 300-day TTL, refreshed on each post). Everything lives in the store, never in code or the CDN, so deploys and headline edits can't touch it; a comment keeps its own copy of its headline's title, so a rewritten or retired headline only greys out its chip. Keys come from `threadKey(week)`: the 2026 threads stay where they were first stored, `ph:talk:{week}`, and later seasons (`seasonOf`: September into the next summer) get `ph:talk:{season}:{week}`. Never rename these keys without moving the data. Anyone reads (`GET ?week=`, `no-store`); posting needs a session. Identity: Sleeper has no sign-in for outside sites, so each manager gets a personal link from Dan, `/#join=<token>` (after the `#`, so it never reaches logs or the CDN). `POST {action: 'claim', token}` checks the token's hash (`ph:talk:tok:{sha}` → Sleeper user id) and sets an HttpOnly `ph_who` session cookie (`ph:talk:sess:{sha}` → `uid:generation`). `TALK_ADMIN_KEY` (env, 16+ chars) claims as Dan (`ADMIN`, his user id) to bootstrap. `invite` (Dan only) makes a new link for a `MEMBERS` or `GUESTS` id (`DESK_PEOPLE` in `lib/members.js`; a guest such as Dan's cousin Saad, "Ex Player Saad", is outside the league: full display name, an `icon` picture such as `assets/heads/helmet.svg`, a "Guest" tag in place of a team, and nothing else on the site reads `GUESTS`), deletes the old token and bumps that manager's generation, which signs out every device on the old link. The server always files a comment under the session's user id; the page shows the `MEMBERS` first name and the team's current name. `post` (1–500 chars, cleaned, one per `GAP_S` per manager, `ref` = `{key, title}` of a headline or null), `edit` (author only, within `EDIT_MS`, one minute), `delete` (Dan only), `signout`. Never touches Sleeper. `scripts/test-talk.mjs` covers it. In `src/hero/Talk.jsx`: `Talk` (`src/hero/Talk.jsx`) sits in the `.hero` of the rankings and Live pages (not in the demo or the preview, `deskShown()`); with the store on, `.hero.with-talk` puts the carousel across both rows on the left (`align-self: start` keeps it square) and the desk under the page head on the right, scrolling inside itself (`.talk-in` is absolutely placed); at 980px and below it drops under the carousel. Comments sit in a tray as white bubbles, yours on `--mine-bg`. The header is a title row (Invite links for Dan) over a meta bar (week picker, count, "Posting as" with Sign out). Minor actions (Sign out, Edit with its seconds, Delete) are small tray pills (`.tc-pill`, `.tc-act`), never underlined links; on a mouse, Dan's Delete sits in the corner of the comment under the pointer. Untagged, the composer offers a dashed "+ Tag the headline on screen" pill; tagged, a solid chip. The first keystroke tags the headline on screen (the carousel's `nav` ref, its index and `go`; keys `w{week}:{type}` from `storyKeys`), and a comment's `On:` chip turns the carousel back to it. The thread on screen is this week's (`talkWeek()`, from the raw snapshot's last scored week, so it turns over Tuesday morning and not when the Monday final call counts the week early); a week picker reads back through earlier threads (`talk.view`), read-only. Refreshes every 25 s while visible (not while a comment is being edited); the draft is React state, so a refresh never touches it. `main()` takes `#join=` off the address bar before routing and `claimTalk(token)` trades it for the session (and picks their team if none is picked).
- `lib/matchup.js` — `breakdown(D, preview)`: this week's lineups slot by slot (the starters set in Sleeper, from `preview.startersA/B`; best projected lineup when a team hasn't set one), position-group edges, and up to 6 prioritised "things to watch" (`urgent` when priority >= 90: a sure zero, an empty slot, a bench swap; `Watch` in `src/games` bolds their first sentence on a flag pip and bolds player names in every line) (starters ruled out or on a bye, legal lineup swaps found by greedy slot-eligible bench swaps, doubtful/questionable starters, biggest edges, close or lopsided totals, the widest floor-ceiling swing player, QB stacks, shared NFL offenses, hot or cold form). The engine's `thisWeekProj` is zero for players ruled out or on a bye (`byes` input), which also feeds this week's win odds. `helpers.player(id)` returns name, NFL team, status, bye, proj, floor, ceiling.
- `lib/trades.js` — `tradeIdeas(D, rid)`: brute-force search of every 1-for-1, 2-for-1 and 1-for-2 swap with each other team (skill positions, value >= 8; about 25ms: per-player lookups are memoized, and the partner's lineup is only worked out once the requester's has cleared its bar). Kept only if the requester's best ROS lineup gains >= 0.3 pts/wk, the partner's doesn't drop and gains >= 0.1 unless they win the value side by >= 3, and value (over replacement, same rule as the trade ledger) is within max(6, 15% of the deal). Roster fallout is modelled: an extra player received forces the lowest-value skill player to be cut; a freed spot adds the best free agent. Max 2 ideas per partner and per incoming headliner. Explanations come from position-group ranks before and after. No AI model involved; it is a deterministic search.
- `lib/members.js` — `MEMBERS`: Sleeper user_id → `{first, photo}` for the league managers, with headshots in `assets/heads/<photo>.jpg` (320px square JPEGs cropped from the originals, about 25 KB each). `photoUrl()` reads `window.__HEADS__` first, which `bundle-preview.mjs` fills with data URIs. A picture that fails to load turns into its initials (`data-initials`) through one capturing `error` listener on the document (`initAvatarFallback`), so renders never attach their own. In `app.js`, `avatar(t)` stays the Sleeper team picture (Dan wants team pictures in the ranking rows, the wire and the matchup cards); `headshot(t, cls)` is the manager photo (falls back to `avatar`). Headshots appear in the headline photos (`Faces` in `src/hero/Carousel.jsx`, with a `STAMPS` label per story type), the manager card at the top of an opened ranking row (`managerCard`, plus `superlatives` badges), the game of the week poster (`faceoff`) and its playoff-drive legend, the busiest manager in the wire stat strip, who shook on each trade card (`dealFaces`), the partner on your best Trade Lab idea (`.pitch`), photo marks in the scatter chart and on your own line in the bump chart (`img`, `faceDot` in charts.js), your face next to the team picker (`paintMe`), and the editor line in the footer. Last place (the "This Guy Stinks" tier) gets the `.drained` grayscale. Keep them sprinkled: one or two personal moments per page, not every list. A new manager needs an entry in `MEMBERS` and a cropped photo.
- `lib/bullseye.js` — money week side pots. `bullseye(D, {week, target})`: during the week, every team's projection (banked plus the rest, same as the previews) sorted by distance from the target, plus `pWin` from 20k seeded draws of who lands closest; the week after, the final scores and the winner; `soon` before; null otherwise. `MONEY_WEEK` in `app.js` (week, target, prize, usual, title, honoree, flyer) drives a lead headline (`moneyStory`, always first while it runs) and a distance chip on each matchup card (`bullTag`). ### Power score

One formula for everyone: `WEIGHTS` in `lib/engine.js`, in Dan's priority order. Standing 40% (`standing` 0.31, place in the real standings: wins then points for; `winPct` 0.09), scoring 28% (`pfPerGame` 0.20, `allPlay` 0.04, `form` 0.04), schedule left 17% (`sos` 0.17), rest-of-season roster 15% (`rosProj` 0.13, `depth` 0.02). `PARTS` groups them; each team gets `t.parts` (standing, scoring, schedule, roster sub-scores) shown as the four bars on a ranking row, and `t.seed` (standings place). There is no admin page or published config: the page never passes weights, so every device computes the same ranks from the same snapshot (`computeLeague({ weights })` is only for scripts). Each metric is z-scored across the 12 teams; score = `50 + 22 * weighted z`, clamped 1–99. `sos` is schedule wins (`t.schedWins`): sum over the remaining regular-season games of P(win vs actual opponent) − P(win vs average opponent), using the same `projMean` and pooled SD as the playoff simulation, so the projection block runs before the power score. The engine also still returns `rosScore`, `sosScore`, `resultsScore`. Bye weeks (`byes` input from `/api/ros`) count everywhere: `weekProj(id, w)` is zero on a player's bye (and this week if ruled out); each team's `weekProj[w]` is its best lineup that week; `rosProj` is their regular-season average; `muWeek[w]` (week-specific strength) drives schedule wins and the simulation, so drawing a team in its bye-heavy week counts as easier. `effWeekly` (lineup display, position groups, Trade lab) is the per-game projection times health times the share of remaining regular-season weeks not lost to a bye. If `/api/ros` fails, ROS terms are dropped (schedule stays, using points per game) and the page shows a "results-only mode" notice. Rank history holds the ROS and schedule terms at today's value, so week-to-week movement comes from results only.

### Week in progress

After Thursday night the current week is partly played. An NFL team counts as played once any rostered player of theirs has a non-zero score in `matchups[upcoming]`. In `computeLeague`: `liveProj(id)` is actual points for played players, else `thisWeekProj`; each team gets `t.live = {banked, rest, left, played[]}` (locked starters' points, best lineup of not-yet-played players in the open slots, share of slots still open). `muWeek[upcoming]` becomes banked plus the blended rest, and `sdWeek[upcoming] = sd * sqrt(left)`, which drives the previews' win odds and the simulation (playoff and title odds). Previews carry `liveA/liveB`. `D.live = {week, teams}`. Season PF and records stay Sleeper's official numbers until the week is scored. The matchup breakdown tags played players FINAL and never suggests swapping them. `buildStories` adds a `dud` and a `star` story from the played games; the `editor` option (`EDITOR` in `app.js`, Dan) makes his own underperforming starter the dud and the lead. Hand-written headlines live in `PUNS` in `lib/stories.js`.

## Design system

Concept: a day game. A flat sky over a mowed football field, and on it cartoon liquid: the cards are one poured body (see `lib/liquid.js`), an inked outline running unbroken from card to card through necks, drips and swells, a pale water face, a crescent of deeper liquid along each bottom edge and hard white highlights. No blur, no soft shadows. Two themes: the day game (light) and the night game (dark), the same stadium after sunset. Every colour is a `:root` token (`--paper` for white chip and input surfaces, since `--chalk` stays literal white for text on the scoreboards; `--header`, `--good-bg`, `--bad-bg`, `--info-bg`, `--hover`, `--notice-bg`, `--mine-edge`), redefined under `:root[data-theme='dark']` in the night game section at the end of `styles.css`, which also holds the few one-off night overrides (the starry sky, the dimmer chalk, chart plates and halos, the tooltip) and the dashboard's own night palette. Anything with words on a bright fill (amber, yellow, lime, the matchup sides, pastel chips) is in that section's dark-ink list, which re-scopes `--ink` back to the day's dark ink on those elements: add a new bright chip there too. `html[data-theme]` is set before the first paint by the inline script at the top of `index.html` (`localStorage` `ph-theme`, else `prefers-color-scheme`); `initTheme` in `app.js` wires the header's sun and moon button (`#theme-btn`, it shows where a tap goes), stores the choice, updates the `theme-color` meta and follows the device's setting while no choice is stored. The dashboard's face-off classes are `pf-` because the Games page's face-off poster already owns `fo-`.

- Palette in `:root` of `styles.css`: `--grass #4CAF50`, `--grass-2 #8BC34A`, `--lime #CDDC39`, `--yellow #FFEB3B` (the first-down line: your team), `--amber #FFC107` (the goalposts: actions, the active tab, featured cards). Supporting: `--sky-1/2/3`, `--board #13301C` (scoreboards), `--turf #1B5E20` (end zone), white cards with `--tile`/`--tile-2` inner layers, `--ink`/`--ink-2`/`--ink-3` (all at least 4.5:1 on white and tile), `--good`/`--bad`/`--flag`, and matchup sides `--a`/`--a-ink` (blue, left team) vs `--b`/`--b-ink` (amber, right team).
- Text sitting directly on the grass must be `--ink` (5.2:1); the muted inks fail on green, so small text there goes on a white chip (`.sec-title`, `.day > h3`, filter pills).
- Type: Big Shoulders Display (numerals, team names, headings) + Albert Sans (body). Tabular numbers on. Both are self-hosted variable fonts in `assets/fonts` (Latin and Latin Extended WOFF2 from Google Fonts, SIL OFL, licence files alongside), declared at the top of `styles.css`; there is no Google Fonts request. `bundle-preview.mjs` inlines them. The team dashboard alone uses Geist (300–400, Latin and Latin Extended), Geist Mono (400–500, Latin) and Inter (400, Latin), self-hosted the same way (`OFL-Geist.txt`, `OFL-GeistMono.txt`, `OFL-Inter.txt`) and only downloaded once the dashboard is open.
- `.glass` marks a card. Inside `main` it is poured (no background of its own; the shape, fill and crescent come from the SVG, the crescent colour from its tone: gold for featured cards, the top three ranks, trades and the best trade idea; `--liq-deep` water otherwise). Outside the shape (header skin, sticky standings, overlays, the kicker) `.glass` is the same material in CSS: `--face`, `--rim` (the inner crescent), your team's yellow ring (`--ring-w`), the ink outline (`--edge`) and a ledge (`--lift`, `--ledge-c`). Your team is the `mine` tone, a pale yellow pool with a gold crescent. The dark boards (`.lead-car`, `.gotw`) are the `board` tone. Buttons are beads: `--gloss` is a small hard highlight up on the left (round buttons carry a smaller one), the old ledge is an inner crescent (`--bead-deep`, `--ledge-gold`), a 2px ink edge on light surfaces, and a press squashes them (`scale(1.05, 0.92)`). Radii step with hierarchy; the panels inside a card use `--pool`, slightly uneven corners. Shading has three steps: the liquid, then a pool inside it (`--tile` #DFF2F8 plus `--tray-edge`: a hard 3px inner top shadow and a 1.5px rim, no blur; used by `.mini`, `.mgr`, `.trade-side`, `.idea-swap > div`, `.bd-side`, `.line > span`, `.fan`, `.fut` and the tab groove), then white chips raised inside a pool (`.mgr-stats > div`). Tracks and hover fills are `--tile-2`, a step darker again. A new panel inside a card gets the pool treatment, never the card face's tone. Nothing rectangular should bleed to a card's edge (the fight poster is inset for this reason).
- The field: `main` is a one-column grid so each page's heading is row 1. `main::before` is absolutely placed from grid line 2 to the bottom and bleeds to the window edges (mowing stripes, a chalk yard line every two stripes); `main::after` is the chalk sideline (hidden on phones). Keep every view's heading as the first child of `#app` or the grass starts in the wrong place. The sky is `.sky` (fixed flat bands plus three CSS clouds, one on phones). The footer is the end zone (`.footer::before` with a white goal line).
- Scoreboards: the headline carousel and the game of the week are dark `--board` cards with white type and yellow accents; the game of the week's graphic is the playoff drive (`playoffDrive`/`paintDrive` in `app.js`): a mini field from 0% playoff odds to the end zone at 100%, one football per team on its current odds, and Today / A win / B win buttons that slide both balls to `playoffIfWin`/`playoffIfLoss` (transform only) with a chalk mark where they started and a sentence spelling out the swing.
- Signature element: each rank painted on the grass like a yard number (white, top three yellow) with a hard offset shadow, half under its card. Keep that the one bold move; keep everything else quiet.
- Charts: marks keep their ink outline with a short hard highlight on bars. `TEAM_COLORS` in `src/charts.jsx` is a 12-colour order checked with the dataviz palette validator on white (every adjacent pair clears colour-blind and normal-vision separation). Labels wear ink, never the series colour; dots get a white ring.
- Copy rules: sentence case, plain verbs, no ALL-CAPS eyebrow labels, no emoji, no "A · B · C" middle-dot strings. Exception by request: last place always gets the tier label "This Guy Stinks" (`tierFor` in `lib/engine.js`); the other tiers are sentence case ("Team to beat", "Playoff push", "Rebuild mode").
- Yellow means you. "You" is either the 4px ring on a card (`--ring-w`) or `--mine-bg` with a 2px `#F2C200` inset on a row, cell or trade side; leaders and winners wear amber (money-week leader row and chip). Reasons and things to watch lead with an inked pip (`--pip`), never a coloured `border-left`. Paragraphs never sit straight on the grass (the Trade lab legend is a white card). Nothing sets smaller than 11.5px.

On phones the source chips in the rankings head hide unless one is offline (the footer credits them).
- The football (phones only, up to 760px): scrolling down past 120px folds the header into a football in its top-left corner (`.navball`, where the logo sits; brown, white end bands, three white laces that double as a menu icon), and scrolling back up 12px or tapping the ball unrolls it (`initBall` in `app.js` toggles `.topbar.folded` and sets the ball's `aria-expanded`; folded, the bar's parts are `visibility: hidden`, which already keeps them out of focus and taps, so there's no `inert` to restyle on the first frame; it goes by scroll distance only, ignores overscroll past either end (the page's bottom is kept by a `ResizeObserver`, not measured on each scroll) and does nothing while `html.locked`). The header never changes size, so nothing below it moves: on phones its glass is drawn by `.topbar::before` (the skin), whose box shrinks onto the ball while the contents fade and the ball spins in over it, and unrolling plays it backwards. The skin's size is the one non-transform animation (scaling would squash the inked outline), allowed because the pseudo-element has no content: measured on a 6x-throttled CPU it costs about 4 ms of main thread a frame with no extra dropped frames, and layout containment, a strictly contained skin box and promoting the bar's parts to their own layers made no measurable difference, so none of them are used. Opening: the inline script in `index.html` adds `html.navball-intro` before the first paint (phones, motion allowed), so the page opens on the ball, tossed in and rocking through the loader; `unrollBall()` takes the class off once the first page is up and the toss has landed (0.9 s after it started; a 15 s timer in the inline script is the backstop). - Must stay usable at 390px wide; check the `@media (max-width: 760px)` blocks (there are two: the main one and a later one for the you strip and opened rows) when adding layout. Page width is `--page` (12px gutters on phones, 20px from 761px). Touch screens (`pointer: coarse`) grow the small pills to 40px. The footer is two columns from 761px (logo left).

Old and new page pictures cross-fade with `mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter` and identical keyframe stops so they always add up to opaque: Safari draws the old picture on top, and any mismatch washes the grass out mid-change. `.tab-ink` is the amber active-tab pill; `placeInk` re-measures on `load` and `document.fonts.ready` because a fast first render can measure the tabs before the stylesheet applies; `reveal()` shows the cards already on the first screen at once (so a tab never opens on an empty field) and uses one IntersectionObserver to rise the rest in and count numbers up (`COUNT` selectors) as they scroll into view; the power rankings have their own (`revealRanks`): the first time a row scrolls into view it rises and grows into place with a slight overshoot (`.rank.pending` → `.rank.arrive`, staggered 70ms within a batch), its yard number pops in a beat later and its numbers count up; rows already seen this visit (`seenRanks`, cleared on each `build()`) render in place, so each row makes its entrance once, on phones too; bars grow, chart lines draw (`pathLength="1"`) and both stay paused until their card is revealed. ## Gotchas

- Sleeper roster `fpts` etc. are split into integer + `_decimal`/100.
- Sleeper team defenses use the team abbreviation as player ID (`"DET"`). Ranking sites spell some teams differently; `TEAM_FIX` in `sources.js` normalises (JAC→JAX, WSH→WAS…).
- Players the sources haven't listed yet (fresh signings) project 0. Patch them in `OVERRIDES` in `lib/overrides.js` (`{pos, wk}`); `app.js` applies it before `computeLeague`, only while the consensus has no `wk` for that player, and the footer lists active patches. Remove entries once the sources catch up.
- Name mismatches go in `NICKNAMES` (e.g. Bam Knight → Zonovan Knight, Cameron Skattebo → Cam Skattebo). Check `/api/ros` → `sources.*.matched` after changes; ~190 of 194 rostered players should have values.
- Scrapers may be blocked from Vercel IPs (browser-like User-Agent is set). A failed source degrades gracefully; don't make the whole endpoint fail on one source.
- Sleeper asks for `/players/nfl` at most once a day; only `api/ros.js` calls it, and the edge cache enforces that. Never call it from the browser.
- Sleeper is called only by `api/league.js` and `api/ros.js`, on the clock: once per morning (for ros: the player map and, approved by Dan, this week's projections), plus the Sunday late pull and the Monday final pull for the league. Dan does not want extra pulls: not on deploys, not for testing, not from new features. Keep reads going through `once()` with a clock key, never add browser calls to Sleeper, and ask Dan before adding any new Sleeper read or pull time. Testing uses the demo snapshot, fixtures and `?at=` (which fakes the page clock only; data keys follow the real clock).
- Ranking data belongs to FantasyPros, DraftSharks and LeagueLogs; this is a personal, non-commercial league site. Keep the attribution in the footer.
- The footer sponsor block is intentional: Dimassi's (struck through, contract expired) and the current sponsor TTP (Texas Truck Parts) with `assets/ttp-logo.webp` (144px, 5 KB, lazy and low priority; re-encoded in headless Chromium from the master `assets/ttp-logo.png`, 84 KB, which the page never loads). The source logo's "TT" letters were transparent knockouts; the PNG has them filled white so they show on the dark background. `bundle-preview.mjs` inlines the logo as a data URI.
- Anything tappable to expand uses the shared `XP` circle in `app.js` (`.xp` in CSS), which flips when its parent is open. Ranking rows say it louder: a drawer pull along the bottom of each card (`.pull` inside `.rank-btn`, "Scouting report", "Hide report" when open). Until a device has opened any team (`localStorage` `ph-opened-rank`), the #1 row is `.nudge`: an amber pull reading "Tap for the scouting report" with the chevron bobbing three times.
